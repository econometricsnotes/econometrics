    Does Ice Cream Drown People?

    Here is a headline I would love to see in a newspaper: "Ice cream linked to drownings — every extra thousand litres sold costs lives." It sounds absurd. Yet the regression behind it would look perfectly respectable.

    About this post

    Concept: omitted variable bias · Data sheet: P1_icecream · Simulated monthly data, 2015M01–2024M12, 120 observations

    Download the data (Excel) — sheet P1_icecream · EViews program

    Let me show you.

    The data

    Imagine a coastal district. Every month we record three things:

    • DROWN — the number of drowning incidents
    • ICECREAM — ice cream sales, in thousand litres
    • TEMP — the average maximum temperature, in °C

    A keen young analyst only has the first two series. She opens EViews and types ls drown c icecream.

    Look at that t-statistic: 20.5. A p-value that rounds to zero. An R-squared of 0.78. If you only read the output, you would conclude that every extra thousand litres of ice cream brings about 0.14 more drownings a month.

    What went wrong?

    Nothing in the arithmetic. Everything in the thinking.

    On hot days, people buy ice cream. On hot days, people also go swimming. Temperature drives both series. Ice cream is simply standing in for the weather, like a witness who happened to be at the scene of the crime.

    Econometricians call this omitted variable bias. When a variable that matters (temperature) is left out, and it is correlated with a variable we include (ice cream), the included variable quietly absorbs its effect.

    The idea fits in one line. If the true model is

    \[ DROWN = \beta_0 + \beta_1 ICECREAM + \beta_2 TEMP + u \]

    and we leave out TEMP, then the slope we estimate on ICECREAM is not β1. It is β1 plus (β2 × how strongly TEMP moves with ICECREAM). Two things must both be true for the bias to appear: the omitted variable must affect the outcome, and it must be correlated with the included regressor.

    Confession

    I generated these data. In the true model, ice cream has zero effect on drownings. Temperature alone does the work. That is the beauty of simulation: we know the truth, so we can catch the regression lying.

    The lesson

    A significant t-statistic tells you that a coefficient is precisely estimated. It does not tell you that the coefficient means what you want it to mean. Before you trust a slope, ask: what else moves with my X, and also moves my Y?

    Something to think about

    Studies often find that people who drink a little coffee live longer than people who drink none. List three omitted variables that could create this pattern. For each, say which way it would bias the coffee coefficient. Leave your answers in the comments.
