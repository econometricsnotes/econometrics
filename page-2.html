The Monsoon Dummy: Switches, Seasons and the Trap Every Student Falls Into

In Kerala, nobody needs an econometrician to tell them when umbrellas sell. The India Meteorological Department's normal date for the southwest monsoon to arrive in Kerala is the first of June, and the rains stay until September. Schools reopen in early June too, so late May brings a rush of parents buying umbrellas, raincoats and school bags.

So why write a post about it? Because umbrellas give us the cleanest possible lesson on dummy variables: variables that are just 1 or 0, "yes" or "no", "on" or "off". They are the light switches of econometrics. And like light switches, they can be wired wrong.

Residuals That Remember Yesterday: Autocorrelation

If today is hotter than usual, tomorrow probably will be too. Heat waves last for days; cold spells too. Moods carry over from one morning to the next. Prices that rose last month often keep rising this month. Time has memory.

When that memory sneaks into the errors of a regression, we have autocorrelation, also called serial correlation. It doesn't make the coefficients wrong, but it makes the regression far too confident about them. This chapter shows how to spot it, test it and fix it, with an electricity company that badly needs to know how much a hot month costs it.
